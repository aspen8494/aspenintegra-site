Welcome to Aspen Integra — my personal research laboratory at the intersection of two worlds I can't stop thinking about: the nuclear backend, and the machine-learning stack that's finally ready to meet it.

## Why this site exists

By day I'm a plant engineer on the Hanford high-level waste mission — melter and off-gas systems, the sharp end of radioactive waste treatment. Outside of that, I build the modeling and planning tools I wished existed: speciated process simulations, optimization-driven mission planning, verification suites that treat mass closure as a contract rather than a hope.

The name is the thesis. **Aspen** is what everyone calls me. **Integra** — intact, whole, integrated — is the goal: one continuous data path from tank inventory chemistry to stack composition and operator schedules, and eventually from plant sensor to machine-learning model.

## What you'll find here

- **Research write-ups** — my flagship models: the HLW facility mission model and the EWG campaign builder, each documented problem → approach → verified result.
- **Blog posts** — what I'm building and learning: modeling techniques, nuclear engineering practice, and applied AI in an industry that generates some of the hardest data problems anywhere.
- **A playground** — small, in-browser interactive versions of the ideas, once they're ready.

The whole site lives in a public GitHub repository — this page itself is part of the experiment. When I push, the site rebuilds and redeploys.

## The thesis, in one paragraph

The nuclear industry's hardest problems — heterogeneous waste, coupled facilities, decades-long missions, zero-margin-for-error physics — are computational problems wearing steel and concrete. AI infrastructure (modern optimization, simulation, and machine learning) has grown up right as that industry desperately needs it. Aspen Integra exists to link the two: to enhance nuclear waste disposal, treatment, and power with the tools that are redefining every other capital-intensive industry.
