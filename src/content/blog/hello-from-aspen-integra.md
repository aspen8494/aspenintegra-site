Welcome to Aspen Integra, my personal research laboratory. It sits between two things I keep thinking about: the nuclear backend, and the machine-learning stack that has finally gotten good enough to be useful to it.

## Why this site exists

By day I'm a plant engineer on the Hanford high-level waste mission, which means melter and off-gas systems: the sharp end of radioactive waste treatment. Outside of that, I build the modeling and planning tools I wished existed. Speciated process simulations. Optimization-driven mission planning. Verification suites that treat mass closure as a contract instead of a hope.

The name explains the intent. Aspen is what everyone calls me. Integra means intact, whole, integrated, and that's the goal: one continuous data path from tank inventory chemistry to stack composition and operator schedules, and eventually from plant sensor to machine-learning model.

## What you'll find here

- Research write-ups for my two flagship models, the HLW facility mission model and the EWG campaign builder, each documented problem, approach, and verified result.
- Blog posts about what I'm building and learning: modeling techniques, nuclear engineering practice, applied AI in an industry that generates some of the hardest data problems anywhere.
- A playground with small in-browser interactive versions of the ideas, once they're ready.

The whole site lives in a public GitHub repository, and this page is part of the experiment. When I push, the site rebuilds and redeploys.

## Why I think this matters

The nuclear industry's hardest problems are computational problems wearing steel and concrete: heterogeneous waste, coupled facilities, decades-long missions, physics with no margin for error. Modern optimization, simulation, and machine learning have grown up right as that industry needs them. Aspen Integra exists to link the two, starting with waste disposal, treatment, and power.
